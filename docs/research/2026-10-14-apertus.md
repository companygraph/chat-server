# Apertus behind the chat — research

> Apertus is the open model trained in Switzerland by EPFL, ETH Zurich and CSCS. Measured through the Public AI gateway with this server's loop and prompt unchanged, the one Apertus model served from Switzerland answered most questions from its own knowledge, without looking anything up in the model, and was wrong or made things up in nine answers of twenty. The newer Apertus is served from outside Switzerland and could not be measured at all. Nothing was built.

Status: measured, not built. Measured on 2026-10-14 against this repository at `a5e97c9`, release 0.37.0, the same commit and the same questions as the measurement of AlpineAI on 2026-10-13, which is `docs/research/2026-10-13-swiss-hosted-models.md`. The probe was throwaway and is not in the repository.

---

## 1. The question

The question was the one the AlpineAI measurement asked: whether a model hosted in Switzerland can answer a visitor's question beside Claude under this server's loop and prompt. This time the model was Apertus, which was also trained in Switzerland and is open, reached through the gateway of Public AI, the Public AI Inference Utility, which follows the OpenAI chat-completions form.

**The measurement says no: the Swiss-hosted Apertus rarely asks the tools, and the newer one cannot hold a conversation through the gateway.**

## 2. The endpoint

The gateway's OpenAPI document is `config/routes.oas.json` in the repository `forpublicai/platform.publicai.co`, and its pages are in `docs/pages` there. The API at `https://api.publicai.co` offers `/v1/chat/completions`, `/v1/completions` and `/v1/models`. A request needs a bearer key and a `User-Agent` header. A key on the free tier is held to 100 requests a minute and starts with USD 2 of credit.

A request passes Cloudflare, then the gateway, then a LiteLLM proxy at `api-internal.publicai.co`, and then a provider the gateway picks per model. Its documentation does not name the providers. The answer's headers do, in `x-litellm-model-api-base`, and on the day they were these:

| Model | Provider | Price per million tokens, in / out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `swiss-ai/apertus-70b-instruct` (Apertus-70B-Instruct-2509) | Infomaniak, Switzerland | USD 0.82 / 2.92 |
| `swiss-ai/apertus-v1.5-70b` | Featherless, outside Switzerland | USD 0.82 / 2.92 |
| `swiss-ai/apertus-v1.5-8b` | Forschungszentrum Jülich, Germany | USD 0.10 / 0.20 |
| `swiss-ai/apertus-8b-instruct` | Featherless | USD 0.10 / 0.20 |

`/v1/models` also listed `apertus-v1.5-70b-thinking` and `apertus-v1.5-8b-thinking`, and a request to either was refused because no such model exists for the key.

The proxy answers an identical request from its own cache, under a header `x-litellm-cache-key`, so two asks of the same question would not be two answers. The probe sent `"cache": {"no-cache": true}`, which turns that off.

Infomaniak refused at times with 429, its own limit and not the key's, in words that ask to contact support for a higher rate level. In the set this happened twice, and each request went through when asked again 15 seconds later.

A gateway module that sends a request's text to an Amazon Bedrock guardrail in Frankfurt exists in the repository, and it is not on the route for chat completions.

### The newer Apertus

`apertus-v1.5-70b` could not be measured through the loop. It refuses any `system` message, even a plain string as the first message, with the words that the system message must be a plain string. Without one, it writes a tool call as text, `<|tools_prefix|>[{"list_types": {}}]`, and the endpoint passes it on as content and not as a call. Featherless refuses every request that carries a tool turn, as `tool_calls` or as a `tool` message.

The probe was made to send the prompt in the first user turn, to read the call out of the text and to send calls and their answers back as text. Even so, from the second round on most requests were refused as invalid, with no reason given. A conversation of three plain messages, user, assistant, user, was then refused ten times of ten while one message went through five times of five. The loop needs a second round for nearly every answer.

## 3. How it was measured

The probe of 2026-10-13 was used again: the loop, the prompt, the host and the meter ran as released, only the model was swapped, and each request and streamed answer was translated as that page describes. Two things were added. Every request carried the `User-Agent` and the cache switch, and every request recorded the provider that answered it. A refusal for 429 was waited out and asked again, up to four times.

The ten questions of 2026-10-13 were asked of the CompanyGraph MCP host, twice each, of `apertus-70b-instruct`. Every request was answered by Infomaniak. Each answer was read beside Sonnet's of 2026-10-13 and beside what the tools had returned; the answer check was not run.

## 4. What came back

| | Sonnet 5, 2026-10-13 | Apertus 70B Instruct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answers a visitor could read | 20 of 20 | 16 of 20 |
| Answers that asked a tool before answering | 19 of 20 | 6 of 18 that came back |
| Right, by reading | — | 7 of 20 |
| English question answered in English | 16 of 16 | 10 of 12 readable |
| Seat, process and KPI over two hops | right, 2 of 2 | wrong, 2 of 2 |
| Requests for the twenty answers | 57 | 34 |
| Pictures drawn | 2 | 0 |

The prompt names the tool that answers each kind of question, and Sonnet called one for every question but the one about revenue, where nothing could be found. Apertus asked a tool for six answers of the eighteen that came back. It answered the rest from what it already knew, and what it knew was mostly invented.

Seven answers were right. Both answers to what CompanyGraph is restated the host's own description, which the prompt carries, without the entity the prompt names for that question. Both tables of processes were right, one of them in German. Both answers about revenue said the model does not say, which is right, and each described a search it had not made. One answer to the model's own question was right on the id and wrong in saying the address stays the same after a rename.

Nine answers were wrong, and most of them were made up. On the two-hop question one answer said the model names no process and no KPI for the seat, and the other named a process, ResponseProcess, and a KPI, ResponseAccuracy, that the model does not have. Both German answers about packs listed packs that do not exist, a product pack and a strategy pack among them, without a tool call. Both German answers about the rules for writing invented a numbered list of rules, R0 to R9, in a file the model does not have. Both follow-up answers said the Answering process is owned by a process. The other answer to the model's own question said no, in German, to an English question.

Four answers were not answers. Both answers about packs were the call the model meant to make, written as text, once as XML markup and once as a code block, and the visitor would have read that. The question to show the meta-model was refused by Infomaniak with a 400 and no reason, both times, at the request after a `list_types` call. Asked three more times outside the set, it was refused once more and answered twice, both times in German.

Requests took 0.7 to 26 seconds, 2.2 seconds at the median. The loop needed few of them because the model so rarely called a tool. In ten of its requests it wrote its plan as text beside a call, which the loop holds back.

## 5. What it costs

The twenty answers used 202,919 prompt tokens and 4,527 completion tokens, which at the listed price is USD 0.18, under a cent an answer, against about USD 0.03 for Sonnet's. The answers are cheap mostly because the model so rarely looked anything up: an answer that asks no tool is one request. Nothing is cached here either, and a request carried six to ten thousand prompt tokens.

Everything the probe recorded on the day, the trials and the smoke runs included, comes to 377,925 prompt and 10,298 completion tokens, about USD 0.34; the direct requests made while finding out why the newer model refused add to that and were not counted. The gateway's billing page was not read.

## 6. What it adds to the earlier page

What the AlpineAI page says a second place would ask of the server holds here as well, and this measurement adds three things.

The place a model is served from is not fixed by its name. The gateway chooses the provider, the newer Apertus of the same family is served outside Switzerland, and an answer says where it was made only in a response header. A server offering "answered in Switzerland" through a gateway would have to read that header on every request, or reach the Swiss provider directly.

Under this prompt the model has to look things up before it answers. A model that answers from its own knowledge under a prompt built on looking things up makes up the very facts the chat exists to ground. The answer check would catch some of it and would see nothing to check where no tool was called.

The newer Apertus would need a way in that takes a system prompt, tool calls and a second round. Through this gateway on this day it had none of the three.

## 7. What was not measured

The sample is ten questions twice, one host, one afternoon, read by one reader. Not measured: the answer check's verdicts on these answers, the two other hosts, `apertus-v1.5-70b` through the loop, the 8B models, Apertus with a prompt written for it, Infomaniak's own API without the gateway, and whether the 400 on the meta-model question comes from the request or from the provider.

## 8. What would reopen this

Three things, in this order. The newer Apertus served from Switzerland with a system prompt, tool calls and a conversation working, through Public AI or directly from Infomaniak or CSCS. Then the set measured again, looking first at whether the model asks a tool before it answers. Then the conditions of the AlpineAI page, a rate a site can live on and a design for this server.
